Services · Tree work

Tree services in Sandwich: five standard operations, done properly.

The mature trees behind your hedge need different work at different points in their life. Marsh-edge sycamores and limes, downland ash and oak up towards Woodnesborough, a leaning silver birch on Strand Street. We do the five that come up on almost every Sandwich garden: pruning, crown thinning, crown raising, crown reduction and deadwooding. TPO and Dover District Council conservation-area applications handled where the tree is protected, and inside the walled town we're careful around the heritage buildings that sit right next to your canopy.

Pruning

Selective removal of dead, diseased or structurally unsound parts: branches, buds, roots. The general tree work every mature garden tree needs on a cycle. Formative pruning on young trees; maintenance pruning on established ones.

Pruning detail & pricing
🍃

Crown thinning

Selective removal of live branches to reduce canopy density. Wind passes through instead of loading the tree, important on the marsh-edge where south-westerlies come off the flats. More light reaches the garden below.

How thinning works
⬆️

Crown raising

Also called crown elevation. Removing lower branches to give pedestrian, vehicle or building clearance, or to open up a sightline. The usual answer when a mature tree has been under-managed and is now leaning on the drive.

When to raise
✂️

Crown reduction

Reducing the overall height or spread by cutting branches back to lateral limbs of a sensible size. Not topping: that's the wrong approach and we don't do it. Reduction protects the tree's future shape while relieving the pressure now.

Reduction vs topping
🪵

Deadwooding

Removal of dead, dying or diseased branches from the canopy. Safety and tree health; nothing structural changes. Standard annual maintenance on established garden trees, especially over paths and driveways. Ash dieback country over here.

Before we cut

TPO and conservation-area checks: included.

Sandwich's walled town sits inside the town-centre conservation area, and there are several designated conservation areas across the parish. Any tree over 75mm in diameter at 1.5m inside a CA needs six weeks' section-211 notice to Dover District Council before work. A tree with a Tree Preservation Order needs a full application with eight weeks' notice. We check both against the DDC map, file the paperwork, and quote knowing which window we're working in: no last-minute stop-work order on the day.

Around Strand Street, New Street and The Quay the canopy often overhangs Grade II-listed walls and jettied timber-framed buildings. We plan drops carefully and always agree access with neighbours before we climb; same principle out on the marsh gardens towards Sandwich Bay, where a poorly-timed drop can churn a lawn for months.
