How to get permission to prune a tree in a Sandwich conservation area: the notice, step by step

There is no conservation area permit to apply for. There is a section 211 notice to Dover District Council, six weeks before the work, at no cost. Here is how to check your address, decide whether the plant is a tree, word the notice and send it.

Updated September 2026 By Richard & the Sandwich Hedges & Tree Services team

It is a notice, not a permit

People search for a "conservation area permit", but in England the paperwork is a section 211 notice under the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. You tell Dover District Council what you plan to do, at least six weeks before, and there is no fee. The council cannot refuse it, but it can answer by making a Tree Preservation Order (TPO). A hedge trimmed as a hedge does not need one; a tree standing in or beside it might.

We look at hedges in and around the Sandwich Walled Town every week, and the question we hear most is "do I need to tell the council before you touch that?" This guide is the answer in the order we do it. It is the application walkthrough; the neighbour-side rules for hedges are in our hedge height and neighbour law guide. Every council link here was checked against the council's own pages on 30 September 2026.

The steps at a glance

  1. Check your address on the council's conservation area map.
  2. Measure the trunk and decide whether it is a tree.
  3. Check for a Tree Preservation Order.
  4. Decide and photograph exactly what you want done.
  5. Write the notice.
  6. Send it to the council, and keep proof.
  7. Wait six weeks, then do the work within two years.
  8. Dead or dangerous trees follow a shorter route.

Step 1: check your address on the council's map

Dover District Council lists 57 conservation areas. Use the council's conservation area map and search by postcode or street name. For Sandwich itself the council's map names Sandwich - Walled Town and Sandwich - St Barts. The other places we cover on the council's list are Ash (three areas: Guilton, Street End and The Street), Eastry, Worth, and five in nearby Deal (Middle Street, Nelson Street, Deal South Barracks, Upper Deal, and Victoria Road and Wellington Road). Woodnesborough and Sandwich Bay do not appear on the council's list as conservation areas of their own.

The council's conservation areas page explains what the designation means. The map is for viewing only, so if your plot is near a boundary, ask the council's tree team to confirm in writing.

Step 2: measure the trunk and decide whether it is a tree

The rules cover trees, not hedge plants. A tree here means any trunk more than 75mm across, measured over the bark at 1.5m above the ground, of any species. Put a tape round the trunk at 1.5m and divide by 3.14. Worked example: a field maple standing in a mixed hedge measures 28cm round. 28 divided by 3.14 is about 8.9cm, so 89mm across, and it counts. A trunk of 22cm round is about 70mm and does not. The line sits at about 23.6cm round.

If the tree has several stems at 1.5m, each is measured and one stem over 75mm is enough. If you are cutting down or uprooting a tree purely to give others room (thinning), the limit is 100mm. Trimming privet, laurel, yew or hornbeam as a hedge is not treated as tree work.

Step 3: check for a Tree Preservation Order

Look the tree up on the council's TPO map. If it has an Order, you apply for formal consent instead (the Planning Portal has an online service for that). A TPO overrides the conservation area rules. Trees on newer developments can also be protected by planning conditions, which you can see in the council's planning application search.

Step 4: decide and photograph what you want done

Write down the outcome first ("clear the footway", "let light into the kitchen"), then the operation ("lift the crown to 2.5m", "reduce height by up to 1m"). Photograph each tree from two sides with something for scale, and mark which is which on a rough sketch of the garden. On the flat Sandwich floodplain soils and in the Walled Town gardens, space is often the real issue, so be modest: a smaller cut that the tree tolerates is better than a large one it does not.

We prune and reduce, so the works you describe in the notice should be pruning or reduction of the tree.

Step 5: write the notice

The law asks for a description of the work, enough detail to identify each tree, and the date. A plan is not compulsory but helps, and one notice can cover several trees and operations if it is clear what applies where. A worked example for a garden in the Walled Town (square brackets are yours):

Example notice

Subject: Section 211 notice, tree at [address], Sandwich CT13, Sandwich Walled Town conservation area

I give notice under section 211 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 of my intention to carry out the following work on [date].

Tree: field maple standing in the mixed hedge on the side boundary, marked A on the attached sketch. Girth 28cm at 1.5m (about 89mm across). Work: lift the lower crown to 2.5m by removing branches up to 50mm across, and reduce the height by up to 1m, cutting to suitable growth points. Reason: to clear the footpath and reduce shading of the patio.

Photographs and sketch attached. Applicant: [name, address, phone, email]. Contractor: [name, phone]. Date sent: [date].

Step 6: send it to the council and keep proof

The council's tree protection page says to let it know by emailing tree.enquiries@dover.gov.uk. Attach the sketch and photographs. The Planning Portal also has an online "Notification of Proposed Works to Trees in Conservation Areas", and the council has published a paper notification form (PDF), although the law requires no particular form. Either way there is no fee. Keep the sent email; its date is the date of the notice. Ask for an acknowledgement.

Step 7: wait six weeks, then do the work

The council can make a TPO, tell you the work can go ahead, or say nothing. If it says nothing, you may do the work within two years of the date of the notice. Do only what the notice describes. Do not start early unless the council has said so in writing. If it makes a TPO, you would then apply for consent.

Keep the notice, acknowledgement and photos, because the council keeps a public register and can check the work against what you wrote. Anyone who arranges or carries out unauthorised work can be caught, not only the owner, so give your contractor a copy.

Nesting season still applies

A notice does not override the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981: it is an offence to damage or destroy an active bird's nest, and the season runs roughly 1 March to 31 August. Plan the six weeks so the work lands in the September to February window, and check the tree for nests first. See checking a hedge for active birds' nests.

Step 8: dead or dangerous trees

A dead tree needs five days' written notice by email to tree.enquiries@dover.gov.uk before the work, except in an emergency. An immediately dangerous tree can be made safe straight away, with written notice as soon as practicable afterwards. Only the work that removes the risk is covered, and it is for the owner or contractor to show the tree was dangerous, so photograph it and get a qualified arborist's opinion (the Arboricultural Association directory helps). If the danger is not immediate, use the normal notice.

Quick answers

Do I need council permission to prune a tree in a conservation area in Sandwich?

You need to give Dover District Council six weeks' written notice, called a section 211 notice, before pruning, topping, lopping or cutting down a tree with a trunk over 75mm across at 1.5m; it is a notice rather than a permit, there is no fee, and the council cannot refuse it.

Does trimming my hedge in a Sandwich conservation area need a notice?

No, trimming plants as a hedge is not tree work, but a tree standing in or beside the hedge with a trunk over 75mm needs a notice before you prune it.

How long do I have to do the work after giving notice?

If the council does not make a Tree Preservation Order, you can do the work described once the six weeks have passed, and up to two years after the date of the notice.

What happens if I prune a protected tree without giving notice?

It is a criminal offence, with a fine of up to £20,000 in the magistrates' court and an unlimited fine in the Crown Court for serious cases, and anyone who arranges the work can also face action.

Where do I send a section 211 notice for Sandwich?

The council's tree page says to email tree.enquiries@dover.gov.uk, or you can use the online notification on the Planning Portal.

Want us to look at the hedge first?

We will tell you which trees in your hedge would need a notice and which would not, and carry out the pruning once the notice period has passed. The notice itself is yours to send as owner.
