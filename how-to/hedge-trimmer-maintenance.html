Hedge trimmer maintenance and end-of-season storage

The main cutting season in Kent winds down from October into November. Fifteen minutes spent on your trimmer now saves a frustrating non-start in September next year.

Updated August 2026 By Richard & the Sandwich Hedges & Tree Services team

Before you handle the blades: safety first

Hedge trimmer blades are sharp and retain their cutting edges even when the tool is off. Before cleaning or inspecting blades:

  • Remove the battery from a cordless trimmer (do not just switch it off).
  • Unplug a corded electric trimmer at the mains.
  • Switch off and let cool a petrol trimmer completely before handling. Hot engines and recently-run blades carry additional burn risk.
  • Wear gloves when handling blades directly. Cut-resistant gardening gloves are adequate for cleaning and inspection; heavier gloves if there is any doubt.
  • Always engage any blade guard or sheath after cleaning and before putting the tool away.

Most hedge trimmers that fail to start in spring were not stored correctly the previous autumn. Gummed carburettors from stale fuel, corroded blades from sap that was never cleaned off, and lithium-ion batteries stored flat in a freezing shed are the three causes we hear about most from customers in Sandwich and Deal. None of them are expensive or difficult to prevent.

STIHL GB's video "STIHL Cleaning Agents | Maintain & Clean Your STIHL Tools" (youtube.com/@STIHLGreatBritain) covers the product-specific routine for STIHL machines in practical detail, including the resin solvent approach we describe below. The steps here apply equally to other manufacturers' tools.

Step 1: Remove debris while the blades are still slightly warm

Immediately after the last cut of the season, before the resin cools and hardens, is the best time to clean the blades. Use a stiff brush or old toothbrush to work out any plant material lodged between the teeth. A damp cloth removes soft debris. Pay particular attention to the area where the blade channel meets the body of the tool: compacted debris here can prevent the blades from moving freely next season.

Privet, laurel and leylandii all leave heavy resin deposits on blades. If you have been cutting these, a resin solvent spray will dissolve the sticky build-up. Apply it, leave for 30 seconds, and wipe or brush off. STIHL's resin solvent is one option; products from Husqvarna, Bosch and other manufacturers also work. Do not use water alone on blades, it promotes rust.

Step 2: Inspect the blades for damage

With the blades clean, check each tooth along both edges for nicks, bends or missing sections. Minor burrs can be smoothed with a fine flat file (see our guide to sharpening cutting tools for technique). A blade with significant damage (bent or broken teeth, cracks in the bar) should be replaced rather than used next season: a damaged blade works harder, cuts worse and can fail unexpectedly.

Check all bolts and screws for tightness. Vibration over a season can work fixings loose, and a blade that shifts during use is both dangerous and ineffective.

Step 3: Oil the blades

After cleaning and inspection, apply a thin film of blade oil or light machine oil to both blade edges. This prevents rust forming over the winter months, particularly important in a salt-air environment near Sandwich Bay and Deal seafront where metal oxidises faster than it would inland. Run the blades briefly (with the trimmer correctly connected to power) to distribute the oil along the full cutting length, then disconnect power again before putting the tool away.

Step 4: Type-specific steps

Petrol hedge trimmers

The single most important step for a petrol trimmer is fuel management. Two-stroke petrol, mixed or unmixed, degrades within a few months and leaves a varnish residue in the carburettor and fuel lines that is the primary cause of petrol tool start failures in spring.

  • Option 1 (preferred): drain the fuel. Run the engine until the tank empties and the engine stalls. Then pull the starter once or twice more to clear any remaining fuel from the carburettor. Store with the tank empty.
  • Option 2: add a fuel stabiliser. Pour a fuel stabiliser (follow the product dosage instructions) into the tank, top up with fresh fuel, and run the engine for two to three minutes to circulate the stabilised mix through the system. Leave this fuel in the tank over winter.

Also: wipe around the spark plug with a clean cloth, check the air filter (remove, tap gently to dislodge dust, replace if blocked or damaged), and wipe down the exterior body. Consult your manual for any model-specific annual service steps, particularly if the machine is several years old.

Cordless (battery) hedge trimmers

Lithium-ion batteries are the most temperature-sensitive component in a cordless trimmer. They degrade faster when stored fully charged or fully flat, and they are damaged by sustained cold (below around 0°C) or damp.

  • Remove the battery from the trimmer body before storage.
  • Charge the battery to 40 to 60 percent before storing. Most chargers will indicate this (typically two green LEDs). A fully charged or fully flat battery stored for three to six months will have noticeably reduced capacity by spring.
  • Store the battery indoors at room temperature, not in a shed or garage where temperatures may drop below freezing. A shelf in a utility room or cupboard is fine.
  • Mid-winter, give the battery a brief top-up charge if it has been sitting for two months or more.
  • Store the trimmer body (without battery) in a dry place with the blade guard on.

Corded electric hedge trimmers

Corded trimmers are simpler to store but the cable is the most common failure point.

  • Inspect the cable along its full length for nicks, kinks, cracking insulation or damage near the plug or tool connection. A damaged cable is an electric shock risk. If you find any damage, the cable should be replaced by a qualified electrician or the tool taken to a service centre before next use.
  • Coil the cable loosely rather than wrapping it tightly around the tool body: tight coiling stresses the internal conductors over time.
  • Store in a dry place with the blade guard on.

Step 5: Store correctly

All hedge trimmers should be stored in a dry, frost-free space with blade guards or sheaths fitted. A cool, dry garage shelf is fine for the tool body (just not for batteries). Hanging the trimmer rather than resting it on the blades is better for the cutting edges. If the manufacturer supplied a storage case, use it: it keeps the blades protected and the tool identifiable when you come to find it next season.

When to get the trimmer serviced rather than just cleaned

Annual home maintenance covers the basics. But if the trimmer is showing any of the following, a service at a garden machinery dealer is the better option before the next season begins:

  • Engine cranks but does not start after running the standard starting procedure (petrol models).
  • Battery holds charge for noticeably less time than it used to (cordless models).
  • Blades move sluggishly or make a grinding sound during use.
  • Vibration noticeably worse than when the tool was new.
  • Any safety mechanism (blade brake, switch) not functioning correctly.

Garden machinery dealers in and around Canterbury and Maidstone handle STIHL, Husqvarna, Bosch and most other brands. In our experience, tools brought in for service in November (off-peak) turn around faster and often more cheaply than those brought in just before the spring rush.
