Hedgehog-friendly hedge management in Kent

The practical steps: where to cut a highway gap, how to manage the base, and the one period of the year when you should leave the ground around your hedge completely alone.

Updated September 2026 By Richard & the Sandwich Hedges & Tree Services team

Hedgehog numbers in the UK have fallen sharply over the past 50 years. The main cause is habitat fragmentation: hedgehogs need to travel a kilometre or two each night to forage, and a patchwork of sealed-off gardens makes that impossible. Your hedge has a direct role to play, and not just in what species you plant. How you manage the base, what you leave on the ground, and where you put a gap in an adjacent fence all make a measurable difference.

The Royal Horticultural Society has published practical guidance on this, including a video by their advisory team on how gardeners can help hedgehog populations. The steps below draw on that guidance and on what we see working in practice in gardens across Sandwich, Deal, Worth and Woodnesborough.

Nesting season and the law

The UK Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 protects hedgehogs as well as nesting birds. It is an offence to intentionally kill or injure a hedgehog. Nesting season for birds runs from 1 March to 31 August: do not cut a hedge during this period without checking it is empty first. The safe main cutting window is September to February. See our full guide at wildlife-friendly hedge trimming for the detail on checking a hedge before you cut.

Before you start: safety

  • PPE: eye protection, sturdy gloves and ear defenders if using a powered trimmer.
  • Ladders: avoid where possible. A pole/long-reach trimmer lets you work the top of most hedges from the ground. If a ladder is needed, have someone footing it and never overreach sideways.
  • Electrical tools: always use an RCD adaptor between the socket and any corded trimmer. Check the cable before starting.
  • Wasp nests and brambles: check the base of the hedge before strimming or clearing, especially in summer and early autumn.

Step 1: Cut a highway gap where it matters most

A hedgehog highway is a 13cm x 13cm gap at ground level in a fence, wall or gate. Hedgehogs naturally follow hedge lines to travel between gardens. When they reach a solid fence at the boundary, they are stopped completely. A gap at the point where the fence meets the hedge line restores that route.

A few things to get right:

  • The gap goes in the fence or wall, not through the hedge itself. Cutting through a mature hedge to create a gap causes more harm than good.
  • Keep it at ground level with no lip or step. Hedgehogs cannot easily climb, and even a small raised threshold will stop them.
  • 13cm x 13cm is the recommended size: enough for a hedgehog, small enough to keep rabbits out of most gardens.
  • Coordinate with neighbours if you can. A gap on your side only helps if there is a corresponding gap on the other side.

In Sandwich Bay, Eastry and the older village gardens of Worth and Ash, many boundaries are a mix of hedge sections and panel fencing. These junctions are exactly where gaps are most needed, and often where they are easiest to fit without affecting the look of the garden.

Step 2: Manage the hedge base for foraging and shelter

A completely raked-out hedge base is of very little use to a hedgehog. Beetles, slugs, earthworms and woodlice concentrate in leaf litter and loose woody material at the base of a hedge. Remove all of that and you remove the hedgehog's food supply as well as its shelter.

You do not need to leave the entire garden rough. A strip of undisturbed leaf litter and loose material along the hedge base, even if it is only 30 to 50cm deep, provides meaningful habitat. If the hedge runs along a back boundary that does not see much foot traffic, you can leave a wider rough margin.

Specific things to avoid:

  • Strimming right to the base of the hedge. Hedgehogs curl up tightly in dense ground cover; a strimmer line can cause serious injury.
  • Clearing all fallen leaves in autumn. A pile of leaves at the hedge base is exactly what a hedgehog looks for when choosing a hibernation site.
  • Laying membrane or hard mulch all the way to the hedge stems. Bare bark chip or membrane gives hedgehogs nothing to forage in.

Step 3: Time your ground disturbance around hibernation

Hedgehogs are active from roughly April to October and hibernate from late October through to March, though in mild East Kent winters some individuals remain active into November or emerge early in a warm spell in February.

The September to February hedge cutting window is fine for the hedge itself. The issue is the ground around the base. Avoid heavy strimming, deep raking or clearing large leaf piles in the base zone between October and March. If you find a hedgehog nested in a leaf pile at your hedge base in winter, leave it where it is.

A simple approach: do the main base clearance in September, when the hedgehogs are still foraging and can easily move away from any disturbance, and leave the ground alone from October onwards.

Step 4: What to plant at the hedge base for additional benefit

If you have space for a rough grass or wildflower strip at the hedge base, this adds another layer. Rough grass attracts invertebrates. Nettles, in particular, support the caterpillars of several butterfly species and the insects that hedgehogs eat. You do not need a formal wildflower meadow: a strip left unmown from April to September at the hedge base, and cut back in autumn, is enough.

In Sandwich, Deal and across East Kent, privet, beech and hawthorn hedges are common. All of these provide ground-level shelter when the base is managed sympathetically. Hawthorn in particular, with its dense low branching and berry crop, is one of the best hedgerow plants for hedgehogs.

Step 5: If you find a hedgehog during hedge or garden work

If you uncover a hedgehog while cutting or clearing, stop work immediately and give it a clear path to retreat. Do not pick it up unless it is injured. A healthy hedgehog disturbed during the day will move on its own given the chance.

A hedgehog out in daylight and looking unwell (walking in circles, lying on its side, covered in flies) is a different matter: contact a local wildlife rescue. In East Kent, Tiggywinkles Wildlife Hospital and the British Hedgehog Preservation Society helpline (01584 890 801) can advise.

The RHS provides practical guidance on all of this in their video on helping hedgehogs in your garden: "How to help hedgehogs in your garden | The RHS" (youtube.com/watch?v=R9LzXCebvwM), from the Royal Horticultural Society's advisory team.
