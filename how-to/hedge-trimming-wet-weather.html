Trimming hedges in wet weather: when to stop, when it's fine

The answer changes depending on what tool you're holding. Work through this guide before you reach for the trimmer.

Updated October 2026 By Richard & the Sandwich Hedges & Tree Services team

Kent gets its share of wet autumn days, and the hedge does not wait. The question "can I cut it in the rain?" is one we hear constantly. The straightforward answer is: it depends entirely on the type of trimmer in your hand, and whether there is disease already in the hedge.

This guide walks through the key decisions so you are not guessing.

Corded electric trimmers: the answer is always no

A mains-powered hedge trimmer is a Class I electrical appliance. Using it in rain, or on wet grass where the extension lead touches standing water, creates a genuine electrocution risk. Always use a residual current device (RCD) when using any corded tool outdoors, and do not use a corded trimmer in rain under any circumstances. This is not a precaution you can weigh up; it is a hard stop. If it is raining, put the corded trimmer down.

Nesting season: 1 March to 31 August

Before any hedge cutting, check for active bird nests. The Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 makes it an offence to damage a nest that is in use. If birds are present, wait. This applies in all weather conditions.

Decision 1: what tool are you using?

Corded electric

Stop. Full stop. See the safety box above. This is not a matter of judgement; it is a legal and safety requirement. Wait for dry conditions.

Battery-powered (cordless)

Battery trimmers are safer than corded ones in damp conditions because there is no continuous mains supply running to your hands. Most modern models have sealed or adequately water-resistant motor housings. That said, rain is still a reason to pause: wet foliage clogs the blade gap, the cut quality drops sharply and you are more likely to jam the tool.

Light drizzle on a battery trimmer is manageable with care. Steady rain is a reason to stop. After finishing, dry the tool fully before charging or storing it.

Petrol

Petrol hedge trimmers have no mains supply risk. You can run one in light rain without an electrocution hazard. Whether you should is a different question, covered in Decision 2.

Decision 2: how wet is the hedge?

The wetness of the hedge matters almost as much as the wetness of the weather.

Foliage soaking after heavy rain

Wait. Wet foliage does not cut cleanly. Hedge trimmer blades are designed for dry or barely-damp material. Wet leaves and shoots compress rather than shear, so the cut edge is torn rather than sliced. The result is ragged brown edges that take weeks to heal over and give the hedge a battered appearance. The blades clog faster, the motor works harder and the job takes roughly twice as long.

Damp after a shower (not dripping)

One to two hours after a shower is usually enough. Check the top of the hedge: if it is still dripping or the foliage is visibly beaded with water, wait a little longer. Once the surface is merely damp rather than wet, a petrol trimmer will handle it adequately. Battery trimmers will too, with more frequent blade clearing.

Thin mist or sea-fret

The East Kent coast gets sea-fret from spring through to autumn: a low damp mist that is not really rain. This is fine for petrol and battery trimmers provided the hedge foliage itself is not saturated. Watch the blades more closely than usual and clear debris more often.

Decision 3: is disease present?

This is the factor that overrides everything else, and it matters for any tool type.

Box blight or photinia leaf spot confirmed or suspected

Do not cut in wet weather. The fungal spores responsible for both diseases spread on water droplets. Cutting in the wet disperses spores further along the hedge and onto neighbouring plants. Spores also survive longer on wet surfaces, giving them more time to find a new entry point. The rule is simple: if the hedge has disease, cut only in dry conditions, and disinfect blades between plants. See our guide to disinfecting hedge cutting tools for the exact method.

Healthy hedge

Standard precautions apply. The main risks are cut quality and blade wear rather than disease, so the tool-type and foliage-wetness decisions above govern whether to proceed.

What to do on a fully wet day

A solid rainy day in October is not wasted time if you use it well.

  • Clean and disinfect all cutting tools (this is easier done in a dry garage than on the lawn).
  • Sharpen hedge shears and check hedge trimmer blades for nicks.
  • Walk the garden and plan the order of cuts: which hedges go first, where the disease risk is highest, which sections need a second pass.
  • Check the hedge for signs of box blight, photinia leaf spot or other issues that you can treat before the next dry window.
  • Book jobs: autumn is the main cutting season and dry days fill up fast.
The one rule we actually follow: if there's any doubt about a corded trimmer in damp conditions, put it away. Battery and petrol are fine in light drizzle with proper precautions. Corded is never fine.

A note on battery trimmer charging after wet use

If you have used a battery trimmer in damp conditions, do not plug it in to charge immediately. Wipe the body and the battery connection points dry, and leave the battery out of the tool for 30 minutes before charging. Most manufacturers recommend avoiding charging at temperatures below 10°C as well, which becomes relevant on cold wet autumn days.
