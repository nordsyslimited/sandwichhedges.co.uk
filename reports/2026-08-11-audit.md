## 5. JSON-LD

**6 issues found.**

### 5a. trim-a-hedge-straight.html - HowTo schema

Uses `@type: HowTo` with 6 explicitly numbered procedure steps. This is appropriate - the audit rule allows HowTo "where genuinely a numbered procedure" and this qualifies. **No issue.**

### 5b. about.html and gallery.html - BreadcrumbList not wrapped in @graph

Both pages declare `@type: BreadcrumbList` directly (bare, not inside an `@graph` array). The established site convention (as used on all service, area and how-to pages) wraps all JSON-LD nodes in `"@graph": [...]`. These two pages are inconsistent.

### 5c. privacy.html - No JSON-LD at all

`privacy.html` has no structured data. A `BreadcrumbList` would be the minimum consistent with other secondary pages (about.html, gallery.html).

### 5d. areas/index.html - BreadcrumbList not in @graph

`areas/index.html` declares its `BreadcrumbList` as a bare type, not inside `@graph`. All individual area pages correctly use `@graph`.

### 5e. All 7 area pages - Service schema missing @id

`areas/sandwich.html`, `deal.html`, `worth.html`, `ash.html`, `woodnesborough.html`, `eastry.html`, `sandwich-bay.html` all carry a `Service` node without an `@id` field. Service pages (hedge-trimming.html, tree/*.html) all include `@id`. Inconsistent - prevents Google from treating the Service as a canonical entity.

### 5f. Most how-to Article schemas missing image and dateModified

Only `when-to-trim-hedges.html` and `composting-hedge-trimmings.html` include `image` and `dateModified` in their `Article` schema. The other 7 how-to Article schemas omit both. Missing `image` reduces eligibility for image-rich results; missing `dateModified` reduces freshness signals.

## 9. Legal Callout Consistency

**2 issues found.**

The reference standard is `when-to-trim-hedges.html`, which names the **Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981** and the **1 March–31 August** nesting-season window.

### hedge-heights-neighbour-law.html - No nesting-season callout at all

This article covers hedge law (the High Hedges Act 2005) and what you can and can't cut. It contains **no mention** of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, nesting birds, or the 1 March–31 August window. Since the article addresses what neighbours can legally do to hedges, the absence of the nesting-season restriction is a genuine gap - a homeowner who cuts their neighbour's hedge based on this article alone won't know they may be committing an offence under WCA 1981.

### renovating-neglected-hedge.html - Nesting birds mentioned but Act not cited

This article mentions "no nesting birds" and recommends "late winter, February or March" as the cutting window, but does not name the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 or the explicit **1 March** start date for the protected season. The phrasing "February or March" is ambiguous - cutting on 15 March could be lawful or not depending on actual nesting activity, but the Act draws a bright line at 1 March. The article should cross-reference `when-to-trim-hedges.html` or name the Act.

Articles confirmed to have adequate callouts: `when-to-trim-hedges.html` ✓, `wildlife-friendly-trimming.html` ✓, `privet-hedge-care.html` ✓, `leylandii-rules.html` ✓, `choosing-hedge-species-kent.html` ✓, `trim-a-hedge-straight.html` ✓.

`composting-hedge-trimmings.html` does not touch cutting timing so no callout needed. ✓
